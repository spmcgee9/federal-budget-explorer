How to read this

Red is money going out of the Treasury and green is money coming in. Darker shades mean more money. On the state map, red states receive more in federal spending than they pay in federal taxes, green states pay more than they receive, and beige is about even.

On the spending and revenue charts, box sizes show money paid out to the public (or gross collections) before offsetting receipts and refunds. The panel shows how each item nets to the official figure.

Offsetting receipts are money the government collects but books as negative spending rather than revenue, such as Medicare premiums and foreign-military-sales payments. Intragovernmental transfers are payments between federal accounts, such as general-fund payments into the Medicare trust funds. Both sides are left out of the boxes so the same dollar is not counted twice, but every total still ties to Treasury's published number.

The Defense Department is published by appropriation title, then by service. Here it is regrouped by service first. "Purpose" groups spending by budget function, which Treasury publishes only one level deep.

State figures lag the budget figures by two years because the analysis takes that long to assemble. They cover the 50 states and D.C. and exclude spending that cannot be tied to a state, chiefly interest on the debt. Because the government runs a deficit, most states receive more than they pay. The deficit-adjusted view scales spending down so the national totals match, which shows which states are net contributors once that borrowing is set aside.
